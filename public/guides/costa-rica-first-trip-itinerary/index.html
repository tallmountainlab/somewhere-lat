Costa Rica has a phrase for everything: pura vida, "pure life." You will hear it as hello, goodbye, thank you and no worries. It sums up a country that abolished its army in 1948, put its money into schools and parks instead, and still feels like the green paradise in the travel brochures your parents kept in a drawer. This route covers the classics, at an unhurried pace.

Getting around

Renting a car gives the most freedom. A four-wheel-drive vehicle is useful for the road up to Monteverde and rural areas in the rainy season. Rental prices often look cheap online, but mandatory local liability insurance is added at the counter, so read the terms carefully. Shared shuttle vans between tourist towns are a good alternative if you do not want to drive.

Park tickets

Many national parks sell entry tickets online through the official SINAC system, and some, including Manuel Antonio, limit daily visitors and are closed one day a week. Buy ahead in high season. A local naturalist guide makes a big difference in spotting sloths, monkeys and birds.

When to go

The dry season runs roughly from December to April on the Pacific side, and it is the busiest and most expensive time. The green season, roughly May to November, brings afternoon showers, fewer visitors and lower prices. September and October are usually the wettest months on the Pacific coast, while the Caribbean coast often has its best weather then.

Budget guide

Costa Rica is one of the more expensive countries in Central America. Plan on prices closer to the US for tours and hotels in popular areas, while local sodas (small family restaurants) offer good-value meals of rice, beans and a main dish, known as a casado.

Memory laneThen and now

For decades, Costa Rica's economy ran on coffee and bananas, carried to the ports by hand-painted oxcarts. Those carretas are now a national symbol, and you can still see artisans painting them in Sarchí. Arenal Volcano erupted to life in 1968 and glowed red at night for decades before going quiet around 2010. Ask a local about the old days and you will hear stories of watching the lava from the road.
