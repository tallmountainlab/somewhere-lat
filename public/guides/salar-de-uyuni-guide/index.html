Salar de Uyuni: the salt flat, the train cemetery and the lagoons

Some places look like they were invented for an old science-fiction paperback cover. The Salar de Uyuni is one of them: a perfectly flat white plain that runs to the horizon, so bright you need sunglasses, under a sky so blue it seems painted on. After rain, a thin layer of water turns it into a mirror, and the clouds appear both above and below you. People have been taking the same silly perspective photos here for decades, holding a tiny friend in their palm, and nobody ever gets tired of it.

The Salar de Uyuni in southwest Bolivia is the largest salt flat in the world, covering more than 10,000 square kilometers at an altitude of about 3,650 meters (around 12,000 feet).

Dry season or mirror season?

  • Mirror season (roughly January to April): after the summer rains, parts of the flat are covered by a thin sheet of water that creates the famous reflections. Some areas, such as Isla Incahuasi, may be unreachable when the water is too deep.
  • Dry season (roughly May to November): the salt dries into a crust of white hexagons, the sky is very clear and all routes are usually open. Nights, especially from June to August, are bitterly cold.

Tours: one day or three?

The salt flat is huge and has no roads, so nearly everyone visits on a 4x4 tour from the town of Uyuni.

  • One-day tour: usually includes the train cemetery, the salt-harvesting village of Colchani, the flat itself, Isla Incahuasi in the dry season and a sunset on the salt.
  • Three-day tour: continues south through the high desert to colored lagoons, geysers and volcanoes, often ending at the Chilean border for a transfer to San Pedro de Atacama. Lodging is basic, and nights are very cold.

Choose a well-reviewed operator, ask about the vehicle, the driver's experience and safety equipment, and confirm what is included.

Highlights

The train cemetery

Just outside Uyuni, rows of rusting steam locomotives and carriages sit in the desert. They are left over from a railway network that once carried minerals to the Pacific coast. You can climb around them, and they make wonderful photos at sunset.

Isla Incahuasi

An island of rock in the middle of the salt, covered in giant cacti, some of them very old. A short trail leads to the top for a 360-degree view over the white plain.

The lagoons and geysers

On the longer tours, you will see Laguna Colorada, a red lake dotted with pink flamingos, the green-blue Laguna Verde beneath a volcano, and steaming geysers in the early morning. These areas are very high, often above 4,000 meters.

Getting there

Uyuni has an airport with flights from La Paz, roughly an hour in the air. Buses also run from La Paz and other cities, many overnight, and a train connects Uyuni with Oruro. Many travelers also arrive from San Pedro de Atacama in Chile by doing the three-day tour in reverse.

Altitude

Uyuni is high, and the lagoon route is even higher. Spend a few days at altitude first, for example in La Paz or Sucre, drink plenty of water, go easy on alcohol and rest when you arrive. If you have health concerns, talk to your doctor before you travel.

Memory laneThen and now

Thousands of years ago, this whole area lay under a series of giant prehistoric lakes. When they dried, they left behind the salt crust you walk on today. For generations, local families have cut blocks of salt by hand and loaded them onto llamas or trucks, and in Colchani you can still see small family workshops drying and packing salt. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, Uyuni was an important railway junction, with trains carrying silver and other minerals toward the Pacific. When the mining industry declined, the trains were left in the desert. Today they are a favorite stop for travelers, and the salt flat that once supplied a few villages now welcomes visitors from around the world, still as quiet and dazzling as ever.

Practical tips

  • Pack: sunglasses, strong sunscreen, a hat, lip balm, warm layers, a sleeping bag for cold nights and snacks.
  • Cash: bring Bolivian bolivianos for park fees, bathrooms and small purchases. ATMs are limited outside town.
  • Entry rules: visa requirements for Bolivia depend on your nationality. Check the official rules well before you go.
