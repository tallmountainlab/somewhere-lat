Cartagena, Colombia: the walled city and the islands

Where to stay in Cartagena, what to do in the old town and how to plan a day trip to the Rosario Islands.

Cartagena feels like stepping into an old novel. It is no coincidence: Gabriel García Márquez lived here as a young reporter, and the city's colors, heat and romance found their way into his books. Bougainvillea spills over wooden balconies, horse-drawn carriages clop through the old streets at dusk and somewhere a radio is always playing a cumbia your parents might have danced to.

Before you go

Colombia asks international visitors to complete an online migration form (Check-MIG) shortly before arrival. Many nationalities, including US citizens, can visit visa-free for a tourist stay. Confirm the current rules on Colombia's official migration site.

Where to stay

  • The walled city (Centro), for boutique hotels and the most atmosphere. Higher prices.
  • Getsemaní, just outside the walls, known for its street art, plazas and lively evenings.
  • Bocagrande, a modern strip of high-rise hotels on the beach. Convenient, though less charming.

Things to do

  • Walk the city walls at sunset.
  • Visit the Castillo San Felipe de Barajas, the large Spanish fortress just outside the center. Go early to avoid the heat.
  • Explore Getsemaní's Plaza de la Trinidad in the evening.
  • Eat fried fish, coconut rice and patacones, and try fresh fruit juices from street stands.

The Rosario Islands and Barú

The Rosario Islands are a protected archipelago about an hour or more by boat. Most visitors take a day trip with snorkeling and a beach stop, or stay overnight on one of the islands. Playa Blanca on Barú can also be reached by road, but it gets very crowded. Choose a licensed operator, check what the price includes (port fees and lunch are often extra) and confirm your return time.

When to go

It is warm all year. December to April is drier and breezier, with peak prices around Christmas and New Year. The rainiest months are usually around October and November.

Staying safe

  • Keep valuables out of sight, especially at night.
  • Use rideshare apps or ask your hotel to call a taxi.
  • Be firm but polite with persistent street vendors.

Memory laneThen and now

The stone walls went up over centuries to protect the port from pirates, and they still wrap the old city like an embrace. Getsemaní was once the working neighborhood where sailors and laborers lived. Today it is full of murals, but its plazas still fill up in the evening with neighbors on plastic chairs, kids playing football and street vendors selling arepas, just as they always have.
